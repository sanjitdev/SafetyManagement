# Habib the Handler — Persona 03

> Priority: Secondary
> Business goals served: G1, G2, G3

---

## Who Habib Is

Habib is a mid-level desk officer at a Dhaka city corporation ward office, in his 30s or 40s. He has worked his way up from a clerk role and now handles incoming citizen complaints — the steady stream that comes by phone, by walk-in, by forwarded WhatsApp, by occasional Facebook mention. He supervises a small team that maintains paper registers, takes calls, and forwards complaints to field crews.

## Psychological Profile

Habib is **civic-minded and quietly competent**. He joined the city corporation because he wanted to serve citizens, and he takes quiet pride in the work — when a citizen's complaint actually gets resolved, he notices. He knows the names of the field crew leaders in his ward. He can tell you which department handles which type of issue.

He is **frustrated by the system he works inside**. The paper-based workflow is the bottleneck he cannot fix on his own. When a citizen calls back asking what happened to their complaint, he often has no good answer — the paper trail stops at his desk, and what happens after dispatch is opaque. He has ideas about how to improve things but lacks the platform to do it.

He **values being seen as competent and useful**. He wants to look good in front of his supervisor and the citizens he serves. A tool that makes him visibly more effective is welcome; a tool that adds friction is rejected.

He is **cautious about new systems**. He has seen government IT initiatives come and go. He is wary of tools that look good in pilot and collapse under real load, or that get replaced in two years. He needs evidence the platform will stay.

## Internal State

When Habib thinks about his work, he feels **beleaguered but purposeful**. He is tired of the paper, tired of the duplicate complaints, tired of being unable to answer the citizen who calls back. Underneath is a steady layer of **civic pride** — he believes in the city's ability to work, and he wants to be part of making it work. He has **measured hope** that the right tool could change his day, paired with skepticism that this one will actually stick.

## Usage Context

**How he finds the product:**
Habib does not discover CityFix — it is installed for him. The platform arrives at his ward via the city corporation IT rollout. He is onboarded by training, not by choice. Early impressions matter enormously: if the first week feels clumsy, he tunes out.

**Emotional state on arrival:**
**Skeptical but interested.** He has seen government IT fail before. He is watching for the first week to see if it actually works under real load.

**Behavior pattern:**
**Daily dashboard-driven.** Opens the queue at the start of his shift, triages by status and age, assigns tickets to field crews, marks in-progress, marks resolved. Filter-and-act, not explore. He spends most of his day inside the queue view, switching to ticket detail on demand.

**Decision criteria:**
- **Speed of routing** — faster than phone-and-paper handover
- **Visibility into field crew progress** — can he see who is doing what?
- **Ability to defend his work to his supervisor with data** — dashboards, metrics, audit log
- **Reduced call-back burden** — when citizens can self-serve status, his phone rings less
- **No new layers of bureaucracy** — the platform must shrink his day, not expand it

**Success outcome:**
His queue goes down each shift. His supervisor sees his numbers and credits him. His call-back rate from citizens drops because they can self-serve status online. He has an audit trail he can point to when asked "what happened to this report?"

## Relationship to Business Goals

- ✅ **Goal 1 — Get things fixed in Dhaka:** Habib is the operational bridge — every ticket he routes is a chance for a fix. Without his active dispatching, Goal 1 has no engine inside the city corp.
- ✅ **Goal 2 — Make the city hear citizens:** Habib is the moment of being-heard. The status trail the citizen sees is created at his hand when he marks "routed" or "in progress." He is, in a sense, the city's ear.
- ✅ **Goal 3 — Operate leanly across cities:** Habib's workflow is what gets reconfigured per city. His daily rhythm in Dhaka becomes the template for Chattogram and Sylhet. Goal 3 depends on Habib's workflow being clean enough to clone.
