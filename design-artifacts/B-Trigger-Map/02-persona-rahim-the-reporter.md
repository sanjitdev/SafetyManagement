# Rahim the Reporter — Persona 02

> Priority: Primary
> Business goals served: G1, G2 (peripheral on G3)

---

## Who Rahim Is

Rahim is a regular office employee in Dhaka, in his late 20s or 30s. He commutes to work every weekday through the same roads and lives with his family in a neighborhood he knows intimately. He is tech-comfortable, uses social media, and pays close attention to the small things that go wrong in his routine.

## Psychological Profile

Rahim is **practical and quietly frustrated**. He cares about his city, but not in an activist way — it's the kind of care that comes from living in a place where small things keep going wrong. He is **proud of his daily routines** — his route, his neighborhood, the familiar corners of his world — and takes quiet ownership of them. He resents that the city around him is not maintained.

He is **deeply skeptical of official channels**. He has tried reporting through the system before and the experience was so slow and unproductive that he gave up. He doesn't trust that anyone is listening. His frustration is not loud; it is resigned, with occasional bursts of muttering anger when something crosses a threshold (the dark street at night, the stray dog that bit a child). When electricity allows, he vents on social media.

He **values efficiency and visible motion**. He has a job, a commute, and a family. He does not have time for forms, hotlines, or visits to government offices. If he engages with a system, he expects it to respond.

## Internal State

When Rahim thinks about his city, he feels **chronic low-grade frustration**, with moments of sharper anger (the dark street, the dog bite, the third outage this month). Underneath is a quieter layer of **resignation** — "this is just how it is here." Occasionally there is a flicker of hope ("maybe someone is listening"), quickly extinguished when nothing responds. The thing he wants most, that he can't quite articulate, is **the feeling that being heard actually means something**.

## Usage Context

**How he finds the product:**
Rahim hears about CityFix from news coverage, social media (Facebook, viral city-corp campaigns), and word of mouth. The government plays a real role in popularizing it — billboards, ward commissioner announcements, integrated city-corp communications. Onboarding is usually triggered by a specific moment of frustration he wants to act on.

**Emotional state on arrival:**
He arrives **frustrated, skeptical, looking for a low-effort path**. He has tried this before with official channels and been disappointed. He expects nothing — but is willing to give this one a shot because it's free, fast, and doesn't require leaving his commute.

**Behavior pattern:**
**In-the-moment reporter.** Rahim opens the app when he sees the problem, not later. He drops a pin where he's standing, takes a photo, picks a category, hits submit. The whole flow takes under a minute.

**Power-user path:** Once his first report lands and shows visible status, Rahim transforms. He becomes a *concerned citizen watching city wellness*. He opens the app 10+ times a day — checking his own reports, scrolling the activity feed, watching the map, seeing what others are reporting and what is getting fixed. He does this partly from civic pride, partly to make sure the system is really working, partly because watching the city move is now part of his routine.

**Decision criteria:**
- **Speed of submission** (under 60 seconds, no forms)
- **Visible status immediately after** (confirms the system isn't a black hole)
- **City activity feed** (proof that other citizens are using it and the platform is alive)
- **Ward-level map view** (proof that his area is being watched)
- **No data entry burden** — name, location, category, photo, submit; nothing else

**Success outcome:**
His report is acknowledged within seconds (status trail created), routed visibly within minutes, and starts moving within days. He does not need the fix to land instantly — he needs **proof that motion has begun**. Once he sees that, the second and third reports become easy.

## Relationship to Business Goals

- ✅ **Goal 1 — Get things fixed in Dhaka:** Rahim is the primary signal source. Every report he submits is a candidate for routing and resolution. His 10+ daily opens of the activity feed and map also drive engagement metrics that justify the platform's existence to the city corporation. Without Rahim and citizens like him, Goal 1 has no input.
- ✅ **Goal 2 — Make the city hear citizens:** Rahim is the acid test of being-heard. He is the user who was previously failed by official channels. If the status trail is fast and visible, he is evidence the platform restores trust. If the system is slow or silent, he bounces back to cynicism immediately.
- ✅ **Goal 3 — Operate leanly across cities:** Rahim is peripheral to this goal — Goal 3 is mostly about authority onboarding and configurability. But the pattern of his engagement (in-the-moment reporting → power-user behavior → civic pride) is what justifies CityFix's existence in each new city, so Goal 3 depends on Goal 1 being demonstrated by Rahim-class users in city #1.
