# Rashid the Resolver — Persona 04

> Priority: Tertiary
> Business goals served: G1 (primary), G2 (indirect), G3 (clone-ability dependence)

---

## Who Rashid Is

Rashid is a field crew member for a Dhaka city corporation — likely a road-repair worker, lighting crew member, or sanitation team member, depending on which department he serves. He is in his 20s or 30s, works shifts, and is at ease with smartphones for navigation and WhatsApp. He has practical, hands-on expertise — he knows how to fix the thing, even if he doesn't always know the formal procedure for reporting it.

## Psychological Profile

Rashid is **proud of his craft and wants to be seen doing real work**. He is the city corp's physical hand. When the work goes well, when he fixes a problem and the neighborhood visibly improves, he notices and feels ownership. When the work goes badly — vague instructions, half the day wasted driving to the wrong place, the fix undone by the next storm — he is frustrated in a quiet, hands-shrugging way.

He is **skeptical of paperwork and forms**. He has filled out paper job sheets that no one ever reads. He has had supervisors who insisted on documentation that added nothing to his day. He expects new systems to be the same.

He **values clear instructions and trust from the office**. When the dispatcher gives him a clear address, a photo, and the actual category of problem, his day is efficient. When the dispatcher sends him on a wild goose chase, his day is wasted. He is loyal to dispatchers who treat his time as real time.

He is **not civic-mission-driven in a Habib way** — he is not in this job for a vision of a better Dhaka. He is in this job because it pays. But he has the *capacity* to take civic pride when the work lets him. Give him the right tool and he will rise to it; give him paperwork and he will perform the bare minimum.

## Internal State

When Rashid thinks about his work, he feels **task-focused with quiet fatigue**. He wants the next job to be clear, the next drive to be worth it, the next fix to feel like real progress. He is wary of being made to look bad by missing details, and he is wary of being used as the "last mile" while the office celebrates. When the system works, he feels **competent and useful**; when it doesn't, he feels **used**.

## Usage Context

**How he finds the product:**
Rashid does not "discover" the app — it is assigned to him. He gets a phone or installs the app as part of the city corp IT rollout. Onboarding is brief and informal; he learns on the job.

**Emotional state on arrival:**
**Practical and unmoved.** He has seen plenty of apps come and go. He will use it if it makes his day shorter. He won't use it if it adds clicks.

**Behavior pattern:**
**Task-driven, mobile-first, low-bandwidth-tolerant.** Opens the app, sees new assignment, sees photo + map, drives there, marks in-progress, takes after-photo, marks resolved. Repeat. He does not browse dashboards, does not read reports, does not log in unless he has to.

**Decision criteria:**
- **Speed per assignment** — under 30 seconds from new-push to driving
- **Reliable on slow signal** — works offline or in poor reception
- **Photo + map upfront** — everything he needs in the first screen
- **One-tap status changes** — in-progress, resolved, blocked
- **No forms, no free-text fields** — tap and go

**Success outcome:**
His shifts end on time. His completions are visible to the dispatcher. He has proof of the work he did. He can drive to the next job directly from the previous one.

## Relationship to Business Goals

- ✅ **Goal 1 — Get things fixed in Dhaka:** Rashid is the physical execution — Habib routes, Rashid fixes. Without Rashid actually showing up and completing the work, Goal 1 doesn't close. He is the only persona who physically fixes things.
- ✅ **Goal 2 — Make the city hear citizens:** Rashid plays an indirect role — when he marks resolved with an after-photo, the citizen's "I was heard" loop closes with proof. He is the visible motion that Goal 2 depends on.
- ✅ **Goal 3 — Operate leanly across cities:** Rashid's mobile workflow is the most fragile part of the platform — low bandwidth, outdoor use, must work in every ward. Per-city success depends on Rashid's app working in field conditions, so Goal 3 hinges on his UX being solid enough to clone.
